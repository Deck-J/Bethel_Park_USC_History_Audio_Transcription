# Gertrude Tanner — lecture on Brookside and the women's club — transcription record

Recording stem: `Gertrude Tanner Lecture`. Every file in this folder carries that stem; this note is the
working record behind the document.

## What this is

A **20 m 13 s (1,212.84 s) talk**, given by **Gertrude Tanner** to the women's club of Brookside, in
Brookside (Bethel Park / Upper St. Clair), Pennsylvania, on the club's sixtieth anniversary. It is a
talk rather than an interview or a lecture in the academic sense: one voice, at the front of the room,
with a librarian's introduction at the head and no questions afterwards.

Three subjects, in order:

1. **Brookside before it was a suburb** — the Indian trail and the fort on old Fort Couch Road, Mr.
   Couch and the land for the fort, his son Nathan Couch's log cabin and stone house on what became
   the Martin farm, the Otts on Kennebec Road, the 1750 dating of the fort, Bethel Church organized
   about 1776 "over in the stone manse", the later church on Bethel Hill, the Freehold Real Estate
   Company of Pittsburgh buying parcels and whole farms about 1900 when a streetcar or interurban
   from Castle Shannon to Washington was talked of, the naming of the streets for Indian tribes
   (Kennebec, Arapahoe, Seneca), and the boulevard.
2. **Her own arrival** — a bride in 1923, the carriage house she and Charles rented on 1 August 1923,
   the pigeons, the Model T, buying a piece of the next farm (the Walden farm), 62 years in the same
   place without a moving van, Charles Tanner's earth dams for the state (Raccoon Park in Beaver
   County, Shawnee Lake outside Bedford), the Martin farm purchase, and the old stone house the bank
   gutted and rebuilt.
3. **The club** — a book club before it, Mrs. Riggs organizing the first one, Mrs. Edna Cully arriving
   in 1925 and proposing a women's club, the bylaws, the drama and garden departments, the choral
   group, and the men's civic organization that met at the Brookside trolley stop. She is the last
   surviving charter member, and she has the minutes book in her hand as she speaks.

**How it was found.** The file was sitting loose at the archive root as `Gertrude Tanner lecture.m4a`
(23,481,203 bytes, md5 `0b119e0b2683b4bb4d9734e64237078f`), untranscribed, and it matches no other
recording in the archive in length or content. Nothing about the name in the filename was assumed:
the speaker is named on the tape.

## Date — 1985, from the tape's own arithmetic

- **1925 + 60 = 1985.** "Way back in 1925, and it's exactly 60 years old today" (15:47).
- **1923 + 62 = 1985.** "I have lived there 60 years, plus the two in the little carriage house,
  making 62 years" (9:48), after "I had come here as a bride in 1923" (3:26).
- Two independent statements, one year. The filename carries no date, and `2026-10-18` is the
  digitisation date, not the recording date.
- **The opening slate is partly unintelligible.** Five cold passes over 0:04–0:24 return "This is
  the 22nd of May, 1965 / 365 / 22nd and Bank, 365, the … library room". The year in the slate cannot
  be trusted against the arithmetic (1965 would make the club 40, not 60), so the year is **1985** and
  the day and month are recorded as **probable: 22 May**, and the month is shown in brackets in the text so that no reader mistakes it for a spoken word. This is the one place in the document where
  a word is written that the ASR does not return cleanly, and it is flagged in `post_edits` and in
  the unresolved list.

## Who she is — and whether she is Gertrude Heck

**The tape names her.** The librarian introduces her in the third person at 0:07–0:24: "…the library
room of the Bethel Presbyterian Church, with **Gertrude Tanner**. And she's going to give us a little
bit of her early beginnings in Bethel". She then speaks in the first person for the rest of the tape
and never says her own surname.

- **"Tanner" is solid**: five cold passes (beam 5, beam 1, with VAD, at +6 dB, and a second chunked
  pass) all return it. One chunk-level pass returned "Bertram Tanner"; the first name is the weaker
  half of the reading and "Gertrude" is what four of five passes give.
- **Pitch was not used.** This is a single-microphone room recording, and the archive has already
  shown pitch to be useless here (the Valentine panel's two women measured 206 and 208 Hz).

**Is she Gertrude Heck?** *Unresolved, and deliberately not asserted.* The Anna T. Connor Papers at
the Heinz History Center include "the meeting minutes of the Get-Together Club kept by Gertrude Heck",
and the speaker here is a charter member of a Brookside women's club who is holding the minutes book
as she talks. That is a real coincidence of subject and object, and it is the reason this question is
recorded rather than dismissed. But:

- **"Tanner" is not a mishearing of "Heck"** — the two are acoustically unrelated, so the tape cannot
  be read as saying Heck.
- If they are the same woman, **Heck is a maiden name**, and nothing on the tape says so.
- No record of a Charles or Gertrude Tanner of Bethel Park was found in a search of obituary and
  genealogy indexes, so the identification cannot be closed from outside either.
- The club is never named on the tape as the "Get-Together Club" — she says "the club" and "the
  women's club" throughout.

What the tape does establish about her: born in Scranton, Pennsylvania; came to Brookside as a bride
in 1923; husband **Charles Tanner**, a First World War officer, an engineer who built earth dams for
the state of Pennsylvania after the Depression; she was a charter member of the club in 1925 and is
the last one living.

## Source and measurement

- `Gertrude Tanner Lecture.m4a` — ALAC, 48 kHz, 2 ch, 16-bit, 1,212.842667 s, 23,481,203 bytes,
  md5 `0b119e0b2683b4bb4d9734e64237078f`, **unchanged as received** (md5 verified before and after the
  move out of the archive root into this folder).
- **Level, before anything:** mean **−45.5 dBFS**, peak **−18.6 dBFS**, integrated **−39.2 LUFS**,
  LRA 20.9 LU. The 60 s block sweep showed the file is **recorded at two levels**:

  | stretch | mean | peak |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0:00 – 11:10 | −54 to −60 dB | −28.5 to −42 dB |
  | 11:20 – 19:05 | −40 to −47 dB | −18.6 to −25 dB |
  | 19:05 – 20:13 | −65 to −74 dB | room tone only |

  Region A measures **−56.4 dBFS RMS** and **−55.4 LUFS**; region B **−41.8 dBFS RMS** and
  **−40.5 LUFS** — a **14.9 LU step**, arriving gradually between 11:10 and 11:40 as someone turned
  the record level up. A single uniform gain cannot serve both halves: aimed at the archive target it
  would leave the first eleven minutes 15 dB under the second.
- **Stereo: delayed duplicate.** Measured on ten 4 s windows: ch0 −56.4 dB, ch1 −55.2 dB (ch1
  stronger by 1.2–1.6 dB in every window), correlation 0.85–0.99 at a **constant 7-sample (0.146 ms)
  lag**. Equal-weight summation would comb-filter, so the channels were summed with the lag
  compensated — `(ch0 delayed 7 samples + ch1)/2`. The 1.2 dB gap is far too small for weighting to
  matter and too small to justify dropping either channel.
- **Gain only; no denoise, no EQ, no resampling.** Two-region gain: **+36.4 dB** from 0:00 to 11:12,
  ramped linearly in dB to **+21.5 dB** by 11:40, held to the end. Pre-limiter peak on the floated
  intermediate was **+6.9 dBFS**, so a true-peak limiter (`alimiter=limit=0.87`) catches the loudest
  transients — the loudest moment is her voice at 0:18.9 and again at 17:33.
- **Measured after:** integrated **−19.1 LUFS** (target −16 to −19), true peak **−1.1 dBTP**, maximum
  sample −1.21 dBFS, LRA 8.9 LU, both regions sitting at the same RMS (−20.0 dB) for the first time.
- **No clipping was introduced**, and this was checked twice: the first intermediate was written as
  16-bit PCM, which silently hard-clipped the +6.9 dBFS pre-limiter signal; it was rebuilt as float32,
  and the finished master was then tested for flat tops (286 samples at the max, longest run 3
  samples — a limiter, not a clipper).
- **Bandwidth:** only **0.01 %** of the energy is above 8 kHz and 84.8 % sits between 300 and
  3400 Hz — the source is band-limited (a PA or telephone-grade feed), which is why gain adds no
  hiss above the speech. Region A carries a noise floor around −61 dBFS (5th percentile frame RMS),
  so after +36 dB it sits near −25 dB: audible as a steady bed under the first eleven minutes, and
  left in, because removing it would also remove signal the transcriber used.
- **No dead air except the tail.** Speech runs 0:06.6 – 18:58.5; after that the file is room tone for
  74 s (frame RMS −65 to −100 dBFS, nothing within 15 dB of speech level).

## Transcription

- Whisper **large-v3** (faster-whisper 1.2.1, CTranslate2 int8, 12 CPU threads), `en`.
- **10 chunks** of ~120 s, each boundary snapped to the quietest 10 ms frame within ±5 s of its
  target (found at 116.35, 235.06, 355.02, 475.18, 595.07, 715.01, 835.23, 955.33, 1075.87 s), with
  10 s of overlap. Never a whole-file pass. `beam_size=5`, `condition_on_previous_text=False`,
  `vad_filter=False`, word timestamps. Audio decoded by ffmpeg to float32 arrays — PyAV in this build
  rejects the `metadata_errors` kwarg faster-whisper passes.
- **Prompt:** `seed_bethel.txt` (the township alternative) plus the archive's corroborated Bethel
  names, 417 characters, one alternative only, no CSV and no name inferred from the filename:
  Bethel Park, Bethel Township, Snowden Township, Chartiers, Brookside, Bethel Presbyterian Church,
  Bethel Cemetery, Bethel Academy, Oliver Miller Homestead, Stone Manse, Coverdale, Get-Together Club,
  Nathan Couch, Martin, Riggs, Croco, Boyer, Stilley, McMillan, Wycoff, Tidball, Wolfe, McCully,
  Patterson, Douglas, Clark, Woods, Alexander Millar, James Miller, Henry Hulse, John Nevill,
  William Tidball. **The speaker's own name was deliberately left out** so that a self-introduction
  could not be manufactured by the prompt.
- **No prompt leak and no prompt collapse.** The same ten chunk boundaries were re-transcribed with
  `initial_prompt=None` and compared window by window: per-chunk word counts differ by 0–3 words
  (241/240, 238/245, 257/261, 306/306, 300/298, 250/249, 282/282, 258/259, 276/278, 138/138), no
  segment in the seeded pass is made of seed tokens, and no chunk head returned a politeness formula
  over real dialogue. Nothing needed splicing.
- **Two seams lost content in the seeded pass, and both were recovered from the un-prompted pass.**
  At 3:40 the words "brother who was living on Brookside Boulevard" were dropped, and at 13:55 the
  clause "Great big wide oak beams lay right on the dirt, and the walls were plastered with mud, horse
  hair, and lime" — the latter between two overlapping tellings of the carriage-house passage. Both
  are in the document and recorded in `post_edits`; nothing was deleted from the raw segments.
- **Tail hallucinations removed:** three `Thank you.` segments at 19:20, 19:50 and 20:12 sit in room
  tone 22 s and more after the last word, with `no_speech_prob` 0.74–0.98 and word probability
  0.55–0.68, each 0.2–1.5 s long. They are the model's filler over silence and are recorded as a
  deletion in `post_edits`.

## Unresolved (left as heard, not guessed)

1. **Whether Gertrude Tanner is Gertrude Heck**, keeper of the Get-Together Club minutes in the Anna
   T. Connor Papers. See above. The tape's surname cannot be made to say Heck.
2. **The day and month in the opening slate** — "22nd of May" is the best reading of five passes; the
   year is established, the date is not.
3. **The librarian's own name** — "my name is … the librarian" is unintelligible in every pass.
4. **"the main one of the rain roads"** (4:36) — possibly "run roads" (the local run-road names) or
   "railroad"; neither is supported strongly enough to correct.
5. **"Mrs. Edna Cully"** (16:43) — four cold passes agree on Cully; the archive's McCully family is a
   possible source, but "Mc" is never heard.
6. **"I couldn't say the word Raccoon Park"** (12:00) — may be a stumble or a self-correction to
   "Raccoon Creek"; left as heard.
7. **"over at this old stone land"** (3:01) — "stone house" would fit the sense, but that is not what
   the model returns.
8. **"Almeda"** (13:23) — the Martin daughter's given name as heard; not checked against a record.
9. **Charles Tanner's dams** — she says he built the Raccoon Park lake in Beaver County and the one at
   Shawnee Lake outside Bedford. Raccoon Creek State Park's dam is an earth dam of 1948 and Shawnee
   State Park is in Bedford County, but no record naming Tanner as the builder was found. The claim is
   hers and is transcribed as hers.

    "Gertrude Tanner Lecture.txt" "Gertrude Tanner Lecture.docx" \
    --title "Bethel Presbyterian Church" --subtitle "Historical Room - Oral History" \

    --note "<the provenance note as built>" \

    "Gertrude Tanner Lecture.txt" "Gertrude Tanner Lecture.md" \

two arguments and the label is silently dropped.
