# Bethel Presbyterian Church

## Historical Room - Oral History: Edna M. Croco, 13 September 1983

**Edna M. Croco, spoken with Ann Connor | recorded 13 September 1983 | 34 m 20 s**

---

> **Provenance.** Note on the subject. Edna M. Croco, of Bethel Park, Allegheny County, Pennsylvania: born 20 September 1899, died May 1988, aged 88 - 83 on the day of this recording, seven days short of her 84th birthday. Those dates come from a genealogy index entry, which is a SECONDARY source; the primary obituary (Pittsburgh Press / Post-Gazette, May 1988) has not been obtained and sits behind a paywall, so they are not confirmed from a primary record, and should be cited as secondary. The year itself is corroborated from the tape: she says she was "about two years old" when the third meeting house lost part of its roof, and the 1951 history dates that storm to Easter Sunday, 30 March 1902. The same history records "Miss Edna M. Croco" joining Bethel on 31 January 1915 and lists "Croco, J. Wilson" on the roll. - Recorded on Tuesday 13 September 1983, the date she gives in the first seconds ("This is Tuesday, September the 13th, 1983, and this is Ann Connor"); 13 September 1983 was a Tuesday, and nothing in the recording contradicts it. Two voices: EDNA (Edna Croco, the subject) and INTERVIEWER (Ann Connor, named on tape), separated by content rather than by voice - the two women are within one pitch band on a single microphone. Transcribed with Whisper large-v3 in 16 chunks of about 120 s, plus a second un-prompted pass; names corrected only where the church record made them plain (Maits, Croco, McEwen, Poellot, Johnston, Reiter, Snowden, Wilson Road, 1899) and every correction logged in the JSON. Twelve items remain unresolved - see NOTES.

**ANN CONNOR:** This is Tuesday, September the 13th, 1983, and this is Ann Connor.

**ANN CONNOR:** I've just been over at the Miller Homestead preparing for a flower show to be given by the Bethel Garden Club, and I took along with me the prayer that Mrs. Edna Miller Maits gave at the homestead back in 1979. I also took some pictures of the Bicentennial with pictures of Mrs. Maits at the homestead. I'm now headed over to Edna Croco. Tomorrow at the Women's Association meeting at Bethel Presbyterian Church I've been asked to read the prayer that Mrs. Maits made at the homestead, and I'm hoping to get some additional information about Edna's thoughts and memories of Mrs. Maits, to add to that prayer.

**ANN CONNOR:** I'm going to turn it on and you can hear me.

**EDNA:** Well, I remember Edna Maits when she was a young girl. I was her, well, I don't know. I wouldn't say whether she was born over there or not, but my grandmother had property down over the hill from Johnston Wilson.

**ANN CONNOR:** Okay. And Wilson Road. Yeah. All right.

**EDNA:** And they rented that. And I remember going over there when, well, shortly after Mama died, with Daddy, to collect the rent, and then I got to know Edna better with getting into the white of Bible plans.

**EDNA:** And she was one of the members that hung out to the end of the Bible class. There was Blanche McEwen and I forget who else was in there. And we used to have in that class, we used to have as high as 75 members, but they all died and got married, moved away.

**EDNA:** Well, I have a picture of Dr. Wycoff's Bible class, and one of the things that she wanted to be sure that we saved, that was stashed out in the barn, is that table that we have up in the historical room that she said he used in the Bible class as his table, where he put his books and so on. That was one thing she made me aware of.

**ANN CONNOR:** Now, you say that you lived there.

**EDNA:** Well, I better not put this on the tape.

**ANN CONNOR:** I was going to ask you who was going to get this bill.

**EDNA:** Well, I don't know. I don't know anything about that. That's a decision that the Millers have to make.

**ANN CONNOR:** But to get back to your statement that they lived over on Wilson Road: one day I took Edna for a ride in the car, and she pointed out a house that is on Wilson Road as you drop down past from where the Wilson farm was on the hill, and she said that that was where she lived as a little girl, and that she was born in a log house. Did you know that? And she said that the log house is gone, but the other house is still there.

**ANN CONNOR:** Did you know that that was the case?

**EDNA:** I didn't know whether she was born over there or not.

**ANN CONNOR:** Well, she told us that. That's on my other tape, in the ride that I took.

**EDNA:** And then there was three Ednas.

**ANN CONNOR:** Three Ednas?

**EDNA:** Yeah. And there was a man down here at the library. He was a carpenter and he built houses, and his name was Ross Patterson, and he had a daughter named Edna, and he was building the house over to my grandmother, or my grandmother Wilson's, one down over the hill, and he coached them to call her Edna. And then I was born in Edna Patterson, Edna Miller, and Edna Croco.

**ANN CONNOR:** Yeah, three Ednas.

**EDNA:** And the other Edna was, oh, Edna Simmons.

**ANN CONNOR:** Oh, she was one of them.

**EDNA:** No, there was three. Just Edna Maits and myself and Edna Simmons.

**EDNA:** And it was in 1899 when the Simmons house burnt. And Edna, Aunt Maggie, was took Ed, when they took her out, they told her not to look back. That was the 19th of April, and Edna was born the 19th of May. And they didn't want her to look back because they were afraid she would be marked.

**ANN CONNOR:** Who was born?

**EDNA:** Edna Simmons.

**EDNA:** And I'm the last of the three.

**ANN CONNOR:** Edna, you're the last of the three.

**ANN CONNOR:** Well, are there any things that... now, I did talk to you before about remembering when the roof fell off of the meeting house. I wasn't there. I wasn't there. But is there anything that you can recall that would be of interest about that third meeting house?

**ANN CONNOR:** For instance, I have the water cooler that was used there. Now, where was that place, and who was in charge of filling that water, you know?

**EDNA:** Well, I suppose it would be the caretaker, and that was Jacob Morlock.

**EDNA:** And they got the water from the well, what you would know better, down over the hill from where the parsonage was.

**ANN CONNOR:** Who was in charge of flowers, for instance? You remember, you know, even just some of the details of how the church operates. Who brought in the flowers?

**ANN CONNOR:** Was there a special committee?

**EDNA:** I don't think they had a special committee, as long as I know of. They may have after the new church was built, but other than that, why, I think the flowers were furnished by different ones bringing from their gardens.

**ANN CONNOR:** Yes. One thing that I asked to have done at church on Sunday was to ring the passing bell.

**ANN CONNOR:** Do you remember having heard the passing bell, and when was it rung?

**EDNA:** What do you mean?

**ANN CONNOR:** Well, as they pass along the aisle. No, no, no. The passing bell was when someone died and the bell was rung.

**EDNA:** Oh, that means tolling the bell. Okay, that's a different term.

**ANN CONNOR:** When did they toll it?

**EDNA:** Well, when they most surely, if they were brought to the church. I don't remember that being done at Bethel. But I was one that was down the river road. But I don't know, I don't go in for that, because that goes through me like, I couldn't stand that.

**EDNA:** Now, when Uncle Al was buried, he was buried in a, oh, it's Allegheny Cemetery, but it's on the verge of, it's way out.

**ANN CONNOR:** What's on the north side?

**EDNA:** No, it's farther away than that. It's pretty near Butler County.

**ANN CONNOR:** Wow.

**EDNA:** And they told that they'll end up for a while there. And they also have the record of hymns that they sang, that they played, when the funeral procession came into the cemetery.

**ANN CONNOR:** Well, what do you remember about Edna in connection with the church? Now, you mentioned that she was involved in the play for the missionaries inside.

**ANN CONNOR:** What else do you remember that she was active in?

**EDNA:** Ladies Aid.

**ANN CONNOR:** So, Ladies Aid.

**EDNA:** I was not a member of that. That was the one that helped to raise money for the missionaries. Yeah, Reverend Johnston, yeah, in Africa. Then they all done other things for Bethel, the Ladies Aid. I wasn't a member, but I helped serve tables at their dinner.

**EDNA:** Dinner.

**ANN CONNOR:** Did you go to a different school than Edna went to?

**ANN CONNOR:** Yes, you did. What did you do, go to Croco school?

**EDNA:** No.

**ANN CONNOR:** Oh, you went to King's school.

**EDNA:** King's school and Bethel grade school, the 7th and 8th grade.

**ANN CONNOR:** Now, when you were going, where was the 7th and 8th grade? Where were they?

**EDNA:** They were in the seventh grade where I went, was in where they have the arts and crafts center.

**ANN CONNOR:** And Edna would have gone to Wilson. She went to Wilson.

**ANN CONNOR:** She really wasn't in Bethel. She was in what is now South Park. Yeah, Snowden. It was Snowden at that time.

**ANN CONNOR:** And then she taught school.

**EDNA:** Yes. Now, I think I heard them saying over there that night that she taught in, uh, Cur...

**ANN CONNOR:** Mm-hmm.

**EDNA:** Now, I couldn't tell you what school she taught at, because...

**ANN CONNOR:** Well, when did you first know Edna in connection with Bethel Church? Did Edna always go to Bethel Church?

**EDNA:** Now, I might give you some information. She married Rowan Maits, but Rowan Maits was never allowed to join Bethel Church. His mother never allowed him to join Bethel Church, because she didn't believe in some of the things that the Presbyterians did, and she was a Baptist, she belonged to Baptists. Edna used to belong to the Baptist church too, and then she turned, and I guess the whole family joined Bethel, and he was never allowed, his mother was never allowed him to. Joined Bethel Church. He was officer in Bethel, but he wasn't a member of Bethel.

**ANN CONNOR:** Well, I remember Edna telling me that oftentimes during the summer they went to a Methodist church that was up on the hill from where they were, and I laughed at the time, and I said, you were a summer Methodist and a winter Presbyterian, and she said, oh no, she said, my mother was a Baptist, and sometimes she attended the Baptist church, you know. I guess it wasn't in the early time of her life, so she went to several different, yeah, different churches.

**ANN CONNOR:** And then she mentioned that they used to have what they called, well, the Methodist church. Did she say whether that was Gill Hall? I think that's the one she was referring to, because she pointed up the hill and we were down in the valley there trying to find the old Cochran Mill when she mentioned they attended the Methodist Church. So that's one of the older things that says Gill Hall Methodist Church. That's what I thought was rather interesting.

**ANN CONNOR:** And she mentioned also that in addition to the church attendance, they have what they call a cottage prayer meeting. Were you involved in any of that type of thing?

**EDNA:** I used to go to a prayer meeting with Daddy, and leave it over to this old man's one night. And Daddy had been working pretty hard, and he stopped, and when he picked the Bible up, he got a Catholic Bible from sending a coupon in from the morning paper, what's it? Something like that, yeah.

**EDNA:** And here he picks this Catholic Bible up, and when he got over to the Miller homestead, why, Reverend Reiter noticed him going through it and going through it. He says, what's the matter, Ed? Daddy says, I can't find in the Catholic Bible, there's four kings. And in our Bible there's, there's, I think it's Samuel, and then there's two kings. So he was having trouble finding what he was looking for. It was supposed to be the Sunday school lesson that they most generally had at the prayer meetings. And he couldn't find it.

**ANN CONNOR:** Well, that's interesting. I didn't realize that. But I would guess that since the cottage prayer meetings were held in each other's homes, that the Millers would have been a part of that.

**ANN CONNOR:** And was that during the week that that was held?

**EDNA:** It was almost certainly a Wednesday evening.

**ANN CONNOR:** A Wednesday evening.

**EDNA:** A Wednesday evening.

**ANN CONNOR:** And what kind of things were done in the prayer?

**EDNA:** Well, just read the Scripture and had prayer.

**ANN CONNOR:** Was there any kind of a discussion?

**EDNA:** Well, they discussed the Sunday school lesson.

**ANN CONNOR:** And this, according to Edna, the prayer meetings that she attended were attended by neighbors. It doesn't make any difference whether you are a Baptist or a Methodist or whatever.

**EDNA:** Well, we had in our place, why, we most generally ask Mr. and Mrs. Boyer, and several other ones, your neighbors, neighbors. And and this would be largely because of the distance that would have to be traveling.

**ANN CONNOR:** You sort of, it was localized within the areas that was easier to get to. That's very interesting, for today we don't think that it's any problem to hop in our car and travel.

**ANN CONNOR:** Though when the weather would be bad, or were these generally in the evening?

**EDNA:** They were in the evening.

**ANN CONNOR:** So this again would be, you wouldn't want to be out too late after dark.

**ANN CONNOR:** That's a whole different way of doing.

**ANN CONNOR:** And I didn't hear Edna ever doing this, but Ida and Albert told me, when I took care of them, that they walked in bare feet from the Stone Manse to Bethel Church, and they didn't say anything about putting stockings on or anything. They just said about putting their shoes on before they go in.

**EDNA:** Yeah.

**ANN CONNOR:** Now, how many in Bethel Church would do that today?

**EDNA:** Not, I can't think of anybody at this point. I can't think of anybody at this point.

**ANN CONNOR:** Well, I'm trying to remember, you know, one of the things that I think is so interesting about the early history was the fact that they had to depend on either walking or being by horseback. Did you, when you came to church, did you come in a buggy?

**EDNA:** Well, we, uh, towards the last, my, uh, Mr., um, Poellot, uh, John L. Poellot, had the wagon shop in Sodom, and he made, he built surreys, and the two surreys were just alike.

**EDNA:** And Daddy used to devil, uh, one Sunday Grandma Wilson came out and she didn't look where she was going and she started to get into Mrs. Phillips' surrey, and Daddy, of course, with him being as devilish as he was, he let her do it, and then he deviled her afterwards.

**ANN CONNOR:** It's like getting into the wrong car.

**ANN CONNOR:** Well, now you mentioned Sodom. Where was Sodom, and how did it get its name?

**EDNA:** I don't know how it got its name, but it was located down where the 19 goes across, down there below.

**EDNA:** Well, below where George Walters and Mary Walters live, and where that, down in that area there, where you go underneath Route 19 now, where McMurray Road goes on to McLaughlin Run Road.

**ANN CONNOR:** Yeah, I see. So that was, yeah.

**EDNA:** Well, there must be a reason why it's got that terrible name.

**ANN CONNOR:** Well, I think that was one of the things that I think about when I think of Mrs. Maits, and you too, that you remember the, actually, the third meeting house. Were you there when they took it down? Do you remember what the process was, ripping it all down?

**EDNA:** No.

**ANN CONNOR:** Too bad.

**EDNA:** See, I was only about two years old when they had that wind and took one side of the roof off. What Daddy talked about, they had four, um, you know, there's a picture in here, they had four chandeliers or lamps, oil lamps, and he noticed them going up, they were fastened onto the rafters, and when that went up, when the wind would get under that part of the roof, they would go up to the ceiling, and then they'd come back down. And that's when Daddy said that he thought the church was haunted. He always had his way of seeing the funny side of everything.

**ANN CONNOR:** A sense of humor.

**ANN CONNOR:** No, I didn't know that Edna was born over there in that house.

**EDNA:** Well, she told me that she was born not in the house, but a log house, just down from where that house was. And see, Grandma Wilson raised another girl. She was never, they never adopted her.

**EDNA:** But you'd look through books of Grandma Wilson's in the cupboard and you'd find Nellie Beam. Now, uh, Nellie Beam, her husband, her dad drank so, and, uh, different things, and so they took the girl off of them, and Grandma Wilson, uh, put her to old school in Pittsburgh, Academy, I think they called it, but she was never adopted, and she married down a little.

**EDNA:** Ed, um, Ed, um, Smith.

**ANN CONNOR:** From down in Capitana?

**EDNA:** No, that is from...

**EDNA:** Oh, yes.

**EDNA:** She got the morning paper, and I didn't get the paper, and she noticed the death notice when she called me.

**EDNA:** Keep in touch on that, hon.

**EDNA:** Oh, they all make fun of me, because that's about the first thing I look at when I get a hold of a daily paper, as a death notice.

**EDNA:** I unfortunately didn't get to see the write-up on what they said about it.

**EDNA:** Well, partly there is said that there was anything in the paper about when he would save it for me.

**EDNA:** Grandmother. You know, I was always, very often if I was troubled about something, or dealing with a person, or just in my own personal idea, I'd call her, and, you know, we'd just sit down and chatted it, and I was always amazed at how she was able to take charge of the situation, keep a clear head.

**EDNA:** She knew her Bible, didn't she, back and forth.
