**WHITE:** Something else I think would be a wonderful addition to our library would be the Latimers and Sam Green, because both of those books mention Reverend Clark and his part in the Whiskey Rebellion. **INTERVIEWER:** Well, they were available twenty years ago.

**WHITE:** I would guess they're rather rare now. I think at one time I could have gotten extra copies of Sam Green, but I never tried to get any more than I needed myself. So we had a copy of the Latimers that disappeared, but one of the ministers here, when he was living in Mount Prospect Church, handed me his copy and said, "Here, you keep this" — and I'm very grateful to it.

**WHITE:** So I have Shindring, Vladimir's and the Prager. Prager has to do with the Karaski country.

**INTERVIEWER:** Oh.

**WHITE:** It's an exquisite story too.

**INTERVIEWER:** Oh, that would be. And it's rare — it's in book form, but the copy I have is just the newspaper clipping form, and I paste it into a certain scrapbook. That's one of the good brands of cereal in the Observer.

**INTERVIEWER:** Oh, I see. And you just kept adding to it.

**WHITE:** I know she has some material here by Dr. McCartney. I was for years a member of the First Presbyterian Church in town, and every Sunday evening we, as a family, went in to hear Dr. McCartney preach, so I'm familiar with him.

**INTERVIEWER:** Well, this is a Pittsburgh book, and it's not far from Pittsburgh.

**WHITE:** This one I think I like much better than this, right here in Pittsburgh.

**INTERVIEWER:** First — he preached the sermon when they set up the commemorative stone at Hill Church. Were you there?

**WHITE:** No, but I'd like to get a copy of that sermon.

**INTERVIEWER:** If I were — how would I go about finding a copy of that?

**WHITE:** Well, I have it. Yeah, I think I have it. It pays — today I saw it, today in fact.

**INTERVIEWER:** Really? Would you let them borrow it to copy it?

**WHITE:** Yes, sure.

**WHITE:** Here's Morgan's Raid and the Church in the Wildwood. That's a count of his capture out there on a Sunday morning. I just want all of that, to know about it. That's it.

**INTERVIEWER:** Well, I'll have to get that out and read it. We do have a copy of this in the lot, in the public library, but here again I think we should have some of Dr. McCartney's books, because he was very interested in the history — the local history.

**WHITE:** Well, we need to cover the premium now — the second-hand bookstore in there, second story, Wallace, book for concern. He had a number of copies of McCartney there. I'm pretty surprised for him.

**INTERVIEWER:** You mean I can't buy this at the Westminster Bookstore?

**WHITE:** I don't think so.

**WHITE:** I used to have a copy of this that I used when I was teaching school. I used to teach American history, and part of that was teaching Pennsylvania history. And I used the book at school, and when I left the school, I left the book. So I'm without my copy.

**INTERVIEWER:** Well, these two are new from the bookstore a number of years ago.

**WHITE:** And then there's going to be one or two other kids' books that we have. It's sort of drifted in here, but I don't think they can be purchased now.

**WHITE:** Sermons, articles written by the Parliament minister — he was a writer. I've never seen any of them, because I've never seen them in the files, but they ran it back as early as when he was living.

**INTERVIEWER:** How early would you say it would be before they would start using pictures in the Danes?

**WHITE:** The 1860s, I would guess.

**WHITE:** This is from Revelation here. He preached this — this is Revelation, seventh chapter, ninth and tenth person. But here he preached Revelation 2:5. I just hope — I thought he was going to preach the same sermon twice.

**WHITE:** I'm starting to — I got a new bookcase built over at the Crosby Church, and I'm starting to fill it up with books from my collection. So some of these things you might be asking for, that would be a little competition: the Crosby Church member can find two copies of.

**WHITE:** And do you know, I was talking to Margaret Gilfillan. Are you familiar with her?

**INTERVIEWER:** Oh, are you? Well —

**WHITE:** She — in talking to her, I told her that I had found this sermon, and she said, "Oh yes, I have a copy," and I thought I had — you know, find something as long since past, but she had a copy. And I said, you know, I would really love to have a picture of Thomas Espy, since there was so much written about him. And she said, "I have one and I'll send it to you." So she did. So I'm going to have an enlargement of it made, to views in the historical room.

**WHITE:** Well, there's a lot about these Grahams. James Lee Graham, second son of Joseph and Jane Graham — same family.

**INTERVIEWER:** That ties in with your letter then. Oh, boy. Here's yours over here.

**WHITE:** It's a small world, isn't it? How things go together.

**WHITE:** Non-Presbyterian ministers. They have a good index of all ministers in the United Presbyterian denomination — that is UPNA, before the merger with the Presbyterians. And the old book — I happened to get a copy from a lady who here is going to move into the duplex with us, and she was a preacher's daughter, a former preacher here at the UP church in Hickory. And this psychopathic manual, I called it, was gotten out about 1903 and had an index of all the UP preachers and all UP churches up until that time. Well, then, here a few years ago another minister brought that book up to date. So I can go to that and get the name and the location, pastures and all that, for almost any non-Presbyterian minister.

**WHITE:** Now, the Presbyterians don't have that, but two or three historically minded women down there in Philadelphia here within the last few years got busy on indexing all the names and careers of all the Presbyterian ministers they could get their hands on, and they have a card file down there there of all Presbyterian ministries. They claim it's complete, and it's so voluminous that it's going to be a job to get it published, because there's money on them — on the UPNA church, well, nearly so large. **INTERVIEWER:** But does that include just pastors in Pennsylvania?

**WHITE:** Oh, no, no. This is nationwide.

**INTERVIEWER:** I was going to say, I think if you want the information, they would probably be able to — they have this file, I think, alphabetically arranged so that they could find these names.

**WHITE:** There was an old William Jones, associated with the Pigeon Creek Church. I was over there one time, a historical meeting, and there's a man by that name. For some time before he became a pastor — I mean, he was an elder at our church — he may have returned to this area, you know.
