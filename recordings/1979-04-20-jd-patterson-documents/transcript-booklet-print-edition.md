**Recorded** 20 April 1979 · **Interviewer** June Ellington · **Typed by** Mrs. T. R. Stump
**Subject** James Douglass Patterson (born 14 March 1904), son of Richard Miller Patterson and Etta Douglas, grandson of James Douglass Sr., of Patterson Road, Bethel Park.

**June:** I am June Ellington, and I here visiting with J. D. Patterson, or Jim Patterson.

**Jim:** J. D.

**Q.** He lives on Boyer Road, 57 Boyer Road. But you used to live in Upper St. Clair, didn't you?

**J.** No, Bethel. Patterson Road.

**Q.** Now, J. D. you were going to tell me something that happened in school that you just remembered about Irishtown?

**J.** Well, we were talking about history and geography and how it used to be taught. My grandfather, of course, was interested in geography because he had traveled over the United States a lot and had talked to me about different things. She was having trouble getting the class to understand what a canyon was. So, I put my hand up to tell her that I could help out, and I explained to the class what a canyon was as my grandad had told me about it and showed me an illustration of a canyon over in Wilson's woods, which is also in Bethel, at that time.

**Q.** It is true, a lot of children don't understand. Maybe we should go back, and do you want to give me your father and mother's complete name?

**J.** Yes. My mother was Etta Douglas, and my father was Richard Miller Patterson. He was raised at the top of the hill on Brush Run Road, a half mile or so above Peters Creek Church, and mother and her parents had lived around different places. At one time they lived over where the Simpson farm is today, that has the farm market. Then they took back the old Douglas place on what is now ms. p. 1

**Q.** Where was this Patterson Road? What is it off of?

**J.** Do you know Logan Road?

**Q.** Yes.

**J.** All right, you come up from Logan, you go through the arch, and you keep to the right and go on over to Brookside. But if you go through the arch and turn left, that is Patterson Road, and it ends down beyond the Rutherford shopping center.

**Q.** What road is that?

**J.** South Park Road. It crosses South Park Road at the shopping center.

**Q.** Were you related to the Johnston's?

**J.** Yes. My great grandmother was a Johnston.

**Q.** Did she live there in Upper St. Clair? At that Johnston farm?

**J.** Yes.

**Q.** What was her name?

**J.** Oh, you got me there.

**J.** When they had the Johnston history written up, we belonged to the fourth branch. So, I suppose, now I had a copy of that, but I loaned it to Mrs. Connor, you can find it in there.

**J.** I went to Irishtown in my first year of school up until about the last of February. Then we moved to Nebraska, and I did the ms. p. 2

**Q.** Then you went away to college?

**J.** After finishing at Bethel, I stayed home a year, and ran a retail dairy. we retailed milk around Brookside, Hillcrest and Brightwood. Then the year after that I went to California, it was still a normal school in those days, and they prepared you to teach grade school. They taught you what to teach and how to teach it. **Q.** You had some very good teachers?

**J.** Yes.

Then after I had taught six or seven years, I went back for a semester, and by that time it was State Teachers College at California. Then it later became California State College and left out the word Teachers.

**Q.** Let's go back to your childhood. Do you remember anything special about your childhood, like the games you played, or when you were growing up? Do you remember your friends at that time?

**J.** I don't remember too much about the outdoor games. They played ball some, and several games. The Reeds and Detlings and Foliarts were in school then. Roland Foliart was the next farm to ours and he lived where Brookside Lumber is now.

**J.** I started to school at Irishtown the Fall of 1910. Then in March of 1911 we moved to Nebraska. **Q.** You said it was a sod school. Was it straight up and down walls? And just one room?

**J.** Yes. And one room and it had a tar roof. I guess they soaked those planks until they could bend them some, and then they made that for the base and then they laid sods all over with sods across the joints so the water would go out and not go down in, and they were satisfactory.

**Q.** All right. Well, then after you finished college you were a teacher for a while, and did you teach in Bethel Park?

**J.** I taught at Irishtown two years.

**Q.** What grades did you teach?

**J.** Well, the first year I had six grades. Then the second year, there were my sixth grade and a couple of others living around there in seventh grade, and they got into some kind of a fuss ms. p. 5

**Q.** And it ran pretty frequently?

**J.** Yes. There were cars every half hour.

**Q.** But the children had to walk quite aways to get to the car.

**J.** Yes, to get to the car in the first place.

**Q.** You didn't burn wood?

**J.** No. There was just wood provided for kindling to get the fire going.

**J.** Where Brookside Lumber stands today, was the Foliart place. They were very good friends of ours. They did a little bit of market gardening. They took apples and grapes and things in the fall season, and some sweet corn, and tomatoes to Pittsburgh. And, earlier, well, when I was first conscious of them and then during the years we were in the west we kind of lost track of them, of course, being so far away. They also at one time raised quite a lot of strawberries and marketed them in June. Then they had the grapes and apples in the fall. And, I think they had a peach orchard over on that other hill there where Toddy Edwards Brookside Lumber has his big house now.

**Q.** The Hultz's, I think they had a peach orchard on Fort Couch Road.

**J.** I think so too. That was Will Hultz. He had four daughters. Then up here on Bethel Church Road was Harry Hultz, and he had

three sons and one daughter. Mrs. Boyer, near Baptist Road there was their sister, and she had two sons and one daughter, Edith and Lawrence and James Hultz Boyer. **Q.** There are a lot of the Hultz's around yet.

**J.** Yes. Well, now Harry, Albert was his son, and Albert has two sons and a daughter. Earl married a Swem girl and they lived in around one the suburbs of Pittsburgh. **Q.** Squirrel Hill?

**Q.** Did you say the Hayes live over in Houston?

**J.** Dwight Hayes who married the Hultz girl, they live in Houston. They adopted two girls, but they never had any children of their own. George Hayes lives at the Waterdam Road and married Josephine McNary. But they didn't marry until late in life and had no children.
