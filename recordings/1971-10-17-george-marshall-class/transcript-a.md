**ANN:** When I first became aware of the history of our church, I happened to see the big desk that belonged to Dr. Marshall. That's really fascinating, because it's such a large number, and it began to stimulate many questions in my mind as to what type of a man this minister was, where he came from, why he had the type of death that he had.

**ANN:** And while I have been very interested in the early beginnings of our church, I've already covered the life of Dr. John McMillan and our first pastor, John Clark, and Reverend Mr. Woods. The man who has really stimulated my imagination, really gotten me involved in doing research, is our third pastor, Dr. George Marshall.

**ANN:** He has a very long pastorate, almost 40 years, and has covered a very interesting period in our history. He came to Bethel Church in 1831 or 1832, so that he was born in 1803.

**ANN:** So that in his youth he had become aware and was under the influence of the teachings of the very early church, ministers like John McMillan and Thomas Marquis and Elisha Macurdy who was involved in the revival.

**ANN:** So that in his youth he was exposed to these very early fiery evangelistic types of pastors. And then through his own life, lived through two terrible wars — the Mexican War and the Civil War.

**ANN:** And through research that I have done, I was able to locate a very famous sermon that Dr. Marshall preached in the early period of the Civil War, in which he tells of some of the young men who had come from Bethel, or had been students at Bethel Academy, and who were killed in the early battles of the Civil War.

**ANN:** I was impressed with Dr. Marshall in that he had the same desire as John McMillan did, to promote education. And one of the first things that he did as pastor of Bethel was to establish an academy, which was known as Bethel Academy.

**ANN:** And it was his desire to find young men that he could prepare for the ministry. And much of the work that he did as a teacher at Bethel Academy was to encourage young men to go through Jefferson College and become ministers.

**ANN:** We're also fortunate that upon the death of Dr. Marshall, the various items that are in the historical room that I have brought here were passed on to us by the children of Dr. Marshall.

**ANN:** We have furniture. We have their family Bible. We have the pulpit Bible that was used during the period of 1850 to the end of his pastorate, which is the one that I have here, open to Revelation, the seventh chapter, which was a text used when he preached his very famous Civil War sermon.

**ANN:** In research that I did at W&J, I was able to get a copy of the sermon, and I had it duplicated so that it will be on file.

**ANN:** This is one of the things that I hope to do with the historical room, is to bring down some of these important documents and items that have almost completely disappeared, or only be found in historical files — bring them back and make them available to you, so that you can sit down and read this.
