# George Marshall Class (17 October 1971) — transcription record

Recording stem: `George Marshall Class (17 October 1971)`. Every file in this folder carries that
stem; this note is the working record behind the document.

## What this is

A 30 m 23.7 s (1,823.744 s) **class session taught by Ann Connor** on the Reverend **Dr. George
Marshall**, third pastor of Bethel Presbyterian Church — the same man she lectured on in the
c. 2001 Historical Room talk in this archive. She works through his birth in Union County and
education at Milton Academy, his move to Cross Creek to study under Dr. John Stockton, his six
children and the Lee connection, his call to Bethel in 1832, the academy he opened, the bell that
came off the academy and went up on the third meeting house, his farmhouse and the tracts he
bought — and she reads the 1951 history's description of the farm aloud.

It is a **class, not a lecture**. She sets homework ("I gave you an assignment last week"), asks
the room a question and counts the answers ("one, two, three, four — Robbie knows, but I don't"),
hands over to someone else to describe a photograph ("Would you like to tell them?"), and works
from objects she has carried in — the desk, the family Bible, the pulpit Bible open at Revelation
7, a handbell.

**It is the earliest recording in this archive**, six years before the 6 November 1977 needlepoint
talk (John's covering note said four; the archive's own dated list puts the previous earliest at
1977, so the arithmetic gives six). That matters for what is on the tape: in October 1971 the 1977
anniversary talk and the 1983 McMillan lecture were still ahead, Mr. Alvin Dinsmore White was alive
and booked to speak to the class on the 31st, and Ann is still *collecting* — "I had no information
on James Lee at all until I received this notification through the mail".

## Date — 17 October 1971, from the cassette label, with internal corroboration

- **The date is written on the physical cassette**: `10.17.71`. John Decker confirms it is a label
  on the tape, not an inference from a filename. That is the primary evidence and is recorded as
  the recording date.
- **The tape states no date of its own.** Nothing on it contradicts the label, so there is no
  conflict to flag.
- **The label's weekday checks out.** 17 October 1971 was a **Sunday**, and the session is a
  Sunday-school hour: she refers to "as we saw last Sunday with our communion set" and to "an
  assignment last week".
- **The one forward-looking date on the tape fits October 1971 exactly.** At 12:10 she says "on
  the 31st of this month, Mr. Alvin Dinsmore White is going to come back". 31 October 1971 was also
  a **Sunday** — the next meeting of the same class. A label written a year late would put that
  sentence out of joint; it lands perfectly.

## Speakers — two voices, and the measurement behind the split

| Label | Where | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `ANN` | 0:00–26:47, and 29:09 | The teaching voice. She says "the historical room that **I have brought here**", set the class an assignment, and speaks throughout of her own research and her own book. Median f0 **205–254 Hz**. |
| `GUEST` | 0:20:59–0:21:26, 0:27:05–0:29:09 | A **male** voice. Median f0 **123–142 Hz**, measured per segment over voiced frames (autocorrelation, 40 ms window). He interjects twice during the class — a short geographical correction ("It's sort of west of Hickory") and a long, barely intelligible anecdote about a Westinghouse lawyer and the road to Washington — and then gives a continuous first-person account of a missionary career that ends at Bethel: "In 1959 I came to Bethel Presbyterian Church as associate pastor … On May 1st, 1967, I retired." |

**The split rests on measurement plus content, not on the label.** Ann's own blocks in this capture
run 205–254 Hz; the second voice sits at 123–142 Hz in every window. Pitch has been unreliable in
this archive for *Ann against other women* (her range is wide on band-limited tapes); it is
decisive here because the gap is nearly an octave.

**What is not resolved: whether the male interjections and the closing account are the same man.**
The pitch ranges overlap (133 Hz in the anecdote, 123 Hz in the account) but they sit two and a
half minutes of tape apart, in recordings made at different levels, so sameness is plausible and
unproven. They carry one label, `GUEST`, and this note says so rather than inventing two.

**One 26-second passage is attributed with less confidence than the rest.** At 5:24 Ann says
"Would you like to tell them?" and the reply — "Yes, and one of the things is a small picture of
Dr. Marshall, and when we compare it to the one that I have up here…" — is a second person
presenting items she has brought. That voice measures **219 Hz**, inside Ann's own range, and
nothing in the words separates the two women. It is labelled `ANN` because the audio cannot
support a different label. The content says it is probably a visitor.

## The last three and a half minutes are a separate recording

- **A hard break at 26:47.** Speech stops at 26:47; the level falls to −61 dBFS by 26:55, and at
  27:05 a new voice starts **17 dB louder** than the class (block peaks −12.9 dBFS against −20 to
  −22 dBFS for the class). The room tone changes with it.
- Everything from 26:47 to 29:09 is the `GUEST`'s account of his own ministry — a shipboard passage
  of 1951, a year of deputation, Chinese student work in Cambridge, the housing projects near
  McKees Rocks and preaching at Coverdale, eight years as Bethel's associate pastor, retirement on
  1 May 1967.
- It is in the same file and is part of the recording (the tape was evidently restarted, or left
  running into another session), so it is transcribed and labelled rather than cut. Anyone citing
  it should know it is **not** part of the class discussion.

## Source and measurement

- `George Marshall Class (17 October 1971).m4a` — ALAC, 48 kHz, 2 ch, 16-bit, 1,823.744 s,
  44,640,137 bytes, md5 `900a30ad9459a04cbb37fc7e07e04760`, **unchanged as received**. Moved out of
  the archive root into this folder, md5 verified either side of the move.
- **Level: quiet, and measurably so.** mean **−42.1 dBFS**, peak **−12.9 dBFS**, integrated
  loudness **−37.65 LUFS**, true peak −12.86 dBTP, LRA 14.5 LU. Sixty-second sweep: block peaks
  −12.9 to −23.8 dBFS, block means −34.2 to −47.2 dBFS. That is roughly **19 dB below the rest of
  the archive** (−16 to −19 LUFS), which is why it seemed quiet in the monitor. The last 80 seconds
  are digital near-silence.
- **Amplification: gain only, and only on the listening copy.** The master is untouched. The MP3 is
  a two-pass `loudnorm` to I = −18.1 LUFS with a −2.2 dBTP ceiling, measured back at **−19.29 LUFS
  integrated, −1.79 dBTP true peak, LRA 8.7 LU**. No denoise anywhere.
- **There is nothing to denoise.** Energy above 8 kHz is **0.012 %** and 3–8 kHz **0.06 %** — the
  capture is band-limited to roughly 3 kHz, which is tape and cassette-deck response, not hiss. A
  filter could not work here; it would only remove speech.
- **Stereo: delayed duplicate — sum with the lag compensated.** Measured on nine 30 s windows: ch0
  **−43.23 dBFS**, ch1 **−41.26 dBFS** (1.97 dB apart), correlation **0.9866–0.9906 at a constant
  +4-sample (0.083 ms) lag** (raw correlation without compensation 0.883). Same programme on two
  feeds, one slightly hotter. Summed as `(1·ch0 + 2·ch1)/3` with ch1 shifted back 4 samples: an
  equal-weight sum would have added the weaker feed's noise at full weight, and taking ch1 alone
  would have thrown away a near-perfect second copy. Post-sum correlation against ch1 alone is
  0.999, so the sum has not comb-filtered.

## Transcription

- Whisper **large-v3** (Systran faster-whisper 1.2.1, CTranslate2 4.8.3, int8, 14 CPU threads),
  language `en`, `beam_size=5`, `condition_on_previous_text=False`, `temperature=0.0`,
  `compression_ratio_threshold=2.4`, `vad_filter=False`, word timestamps.
- **16 chunks** of ~116–124 s, each boundary snapped to the quietest 10 ms frame within ±5 s of its
  120 s target. **Never a whole-file pass.**
- Audio decoded by ffmpeg to float32 arrays. **PyAV in this build rejects the `metadata_errors`
  kwarg** faster-whisper passes, so the array path is used deliberately.
- **Seeded prompt: the new State Archives package plus this archive's own corroborated names.**
  `whisper_seed.txt` (the general seed) combined with `seed_bethel.txt`'s Bethel/Chartiers
  vocabulary — the class is a Bethel Township subject — trimmed to **195 tokens** so nothing is
  truncated out of the tail. The CSV glossary was **not** pasted into the prompt. `review_notes.txt`
  was read first and none of its uncertain readings (Keykendall, Hampstead, Middleswart, Gilfillin,
  Barrackman, Hewey) was seeded. The package's spelling controls were applied in the fact-check:
  **Miller not Milber, Hultz not Hutz**, and **Millar and Miller kept apart** — neither name is
  spoken on this tape, so no replacement was made either way.
- **A full second pass with `initial_prompt=None` over the same 16 chunk boundaries** was run as the
  leak detector.

### Prompt leak — none found

No segment in the prompted pass is made of seed tokens. The prompted pass produced **3,643 words**
against the plain pass's 3,637, and the per-chunk counts agree within ±8 words except at chunks 1,
11 and 12. **No window was deleted, and no window needed splicing wholesale.**

Where the two passes disagreed, the span was judged on its own words rather than on the count:

| At | Prompted pass | Plain pass | Taken |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2:06 | "So the Indian youth, he was exposed…" | "So **that in his youth** he was exposed…" | plain |
| 2:17 | "lived through two terrible wars, the Civil War" | "…two terrible wars, **the Mexican War and** the Civil War" | plain (prompted dropped a war) |
| 22:52 | "Well, thanks. We're the oldest science team." | "Well, **James would have been the oldest son in the family**." | plain |
| 21:47–23:06 | coherent throughout | 40 words *more* than prompted, but they are invented filler ("he was saying that he knew that there was a place to be young in those two weeks") | prompted |

That last row is the reason a word count is not a quality measure: the plain pass's surplus at
chunk 11 is hallucination, not recovery, and was not used.

### Chunk seams

Each seam was read across after the merge. De-duplication trimmed only a duplicated **prefix**, and
the merged word count equals the sum of the chunk totals minus the three merged fragments, so
nothing was lost at a boundary.

### Quality

- 226 segments, **3,556 words** in the corrected transcript (3,643 raw).
- **Mean per-word probability 0.8609**, **9.77 % of words below 0.5**, 17.6 % below 0.7 — inside the
  archive's acceptable band but the weakest of the eight recordings, and that is a property of the
  source, not the model: the capture has no energy above 3 kHz.
- **Weak stretches:** 7:00–9:00, 17:00–18:00, 21:00–23:00 (two voices, the male anecdote) and
  **29:00–30:31** (the shipboard passage and the silent tail). The 20:00–21:00 reading of the 1951
  history is strong because she is reading a printed page.
- **Read end to end**, all 226 segments and all 90 paragraphs, in order.

## Relation to the other George Marshall recording in this archive

`History of George Marshall and other Ministers/` (31 m 44 s, UNKNOWN date, c. 2001 assumed) is the
**later, expanded delivery of the same material by the same speaker**: the Tidball purchase, the
Hicks family and the farmhouse, the desk in the historical room, the academy, the bell and the
library all recur. Two differences are worth a researcher's time:

- The c. 2001 talk has the **library and the will**, the trunk from John McCabe's barn, the Europe
  trip with Thomas Cadoux and the letter describing it. None of that is on the 1971 tape — she is
  still gathering it.
- The 1971 tape has the **children's names spelled out from Mr. White's letter**, which the later
  talk does not give in the same detail, and it has her saying she had "no information on James Lee
  at all" until that letter arrived.

Read together they date the research: in October 1971 the Cross Creek material was new to her ("I
didn't know anything about Cross Creek"), and Mr. White was still alive and expected in class on the
31st.
